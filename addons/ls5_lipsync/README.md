# LS5 Lip Sync

The **Lip Sync** editor tab edits character voice timing. Runtime scripts live in `runtime`; the editor tab and waveform tools live in `editor`.

The editor screen is created on first opening the Lip Sync tab. Hiding the tab stops playback, pauses the preview subtree, and suspends timeline processing. Character drivers do not poll dialogue or evaluate skeleton poses in ordinary editor scenes; editor pose evaluation is restricted to explicit lip sync preview calls.

## Character setup

1. Create a `LipSyncProfile` resource under the character folder.
2. Assign its preview scene, `VIS_SILENT` pose, viseme and expression animations, and separate bone lists.
3. Add a `LipSyncDriver` under the character's `Skeleton3D`. Assign the profile and the character's `Dialogue_Player` path.
4. Keep the profile and runtime scripts in `characters.pck`. Voice audio belongs in `audio.pck`.

Neo Adventure Sonic has a configured profile and driver. Its viseme mask contains mouth, teeth, and tongue bones. Its expression mask also contains quill and brow bones.

At runtime, the driver matches the stream playing on that character's `Dialogue_Player` against its profile's registered clips and updates the skeleton while the line plays. The online debug dummy keeps its dialogue player, skeleton, and lip sync driver active while the rest of the pawn is disabled. Its voice cycle starts with the profile's registered lip sync clips.

## Clip workflow

1. Open the **Lip Sync** editor tab and select the character profile. The viseme and expression palettes sort by pose ID. After changing pose mappings in the profile Inspector, use **Refresh Lists** to update both palettes.
2. Click **New Clip** or open a saved clip. Assign voice audio after the clip opens. **Save Clip** chooses a file inside the character's folder for an unsaved clip.
3. Enter the transcript and generate a draft. Draft generation estimates pose order and duration from spelling and the detected audible range. Context rules distinguish sounds such as short `e` in “let” (`VIS_AE`) and long `ee` in “see” (`VIS_EE`), and handle common digraphs, silent final `e`, and soft `c` and `g`. Character profile spelling patterns override these built-in rules. The generator does not recognize the recorded speech. Adjust the word spans, then use **Regenerate from Words** to rebuild visemes within those spans without moving the word guidance or changing expressions.
4. Drag the yellow waveform handles to adjust the audible range. Their hit areas select the nearer handle when close together, and the handles remain inside the audio bounds. **Detect Audible Range** runs waveform detection; **Reset Trim** restores the full audio range. Drag word spans as guidance. Visemes and expressions each have four rows; cues may overlap across rows. Use the palette's row buttons to insert at the playhead, or **Replace** to change the selected cue of the same type. Double-click a timeline row to insert there. Left-drag cue bodies to move them in time or between rows, and drag edges to resize. Right-drag from a cue's left half to adjust blend-in or its right half to adjust blend-out. Ctrl+wheel zooms, wheel scrolls, and the snap menu sets the timing grid.
5. Use **Play** to run the timeline. **From Cursor** starts at the playhead when checked; otherwise playback starts at zero. Playback continues silently after the audio ends until the last cue finishes, so lingering expressions remain visible. The **Volume** slider controls editor playback and audition audio. Use **Audition Scrub** to hear short snippets while dragging the playhead. The preview uses the same facial driver as runtime. Mouse-wheel input over the model preview zooms its camera without scrolling the surrounding editor panel.
6. Use Undo, Redo, or Revert Draft while editing. Loop playback uses the selected cue, or the audible range when no cue is selected. The editor identifies the open clip and shows unsaved changes. Save and validate. The editor adds saved clip paths to the character profile and the structured export presets.

The saved clip dropdown resolves Godot UID references to resource filenames. Use **Refresh Clip Names** after changing profile clip references or renaming files. Unresolved references appear as disabled **Missing clip** entries with the original reference in their tooltip.

Selecting another clip opens it in the timeline and audio controls. If the current clip has unsaved edits, choose **Save and Open**, **Discard and Open**, or **Cancel**. Opening a saved clip does not mark it dirty when its trim is normalized to the audio bounds.

Each clip defaults to **Additive Overlay**. The driver applies viseme and expression differences from `VIS_SILENT` over the current gameplay pose after the character's animation tree. **Absolute Override** instead sets the affected facial bones to the clip's pose for its influence period. **Influence Duration** scales cue timing to a chosen runtime duration; zero uses the authored timeline length. At most one cue per row is active at a time; active cues on different rows blend together. Strength sets each cue's influence from zero to full. Overlapping visemes crossfade at their individual strengths while full-strength transitions retain continuous coverage. Expressions default to **Compound**: each row adds its strength-weighted translation and rotation offsets relative to VIS_SILENT. Brow raises and brow angles can therefore combine. Offsets are rebuilt from the gameplay pose each evaluation, so they do not accumulate across frames. Rotations stay normalized; combined positive scales are bounded to 0.25–4 times the neutral scale. Authored poses and strengths still determine whether a combination looks appropriate. Set individual expression cues to **Crossfade** to share influence with other Crossfade cues on overlapping rows; Compound cues continue adding independently. Poses without a track for a bone do not dilute other cues on that bone. Each clip stores separate new viseme (0.04 s) and expression (0.15 s) blend-in and blend-out defaults. Changing these durations affects new cues. Viseme and expression fade curves default to **Smoothstep**, with **Linear** and **Sine** alternatives and a per-cue **Default** or curve override. Default curves affect existing cues that have no override; the timeline draws the selected curve. **Smoothing (s)** controls optional frame-rate-independent facial response smoothing, disabled at zero. New clips created in the editor start at 0.01 s. Existing resources without a smoothing value retain zero smoothing. Playback includes a short release tail; seeking and scrubbing reset smoothing for an exact pose at the cursor. Blend settings are saved, included in undo/redo, and retained by character transfer. Transcript draft padding defaults to zero: consecutive visemes cycle through rows 1–4 and overlap at their blend edges for continuous coverage. A positive padding value adds gaps where the audible range allows. Changing padding updates newly generated cues; use **Regenerate from Words** to apply it to cues already on the timeline. Blend lengths are limited to half each cue's duration. Expression ends may extend past the audio to linger.

Options below the timeline are grouped into **Transcript and Word Guidance**, **Selected Cue**, **Clip Playback**, and **Generation and Blending**. These sections start collapsed; expand a header to edit its controls. Collapsing sections preserves their values and keeps hidden controls out of focus navigation. The Selected Cue header shows the current selection count.

The character profile picker stays in a fixed header above the three columns. Clip and audio pickers stay above the palettes. Each palette list scrolls independently. The timeline and categories share a centre-only scroll area with focus following, while playback controls remain fixed above it. Scrolling centre options cannot move the preview or palettes. The preview occupies a separate fixed column; its wheel input only zooms the camera. Drag the column dividers to adjust their widths.

## Timeline selection

Cues and word guidance clips support the same selection controls. Click a clip to select it, Shift-click to add it, and Ctrl-click to subtract it. Drag empty track space to box-select intersecting clips; Shift adds and Ctrl subtracts. A click in empty track space clears selection. Drag the body of an already selected clip to move the group. Selected clips have blue outlines; the active clip has a stronger white outline. The inspector identifies the selection count and active clip.

Group movement preserves durations and relative timing. Snapping uses the dragged clip's start as the anchor, and movement stops when the earliest selected clip reaches zero. A selection containing only visemes or only expressions can shift between its own rows while preserving relative row spacing. Row boundaries limit the whole group. Words remain on their guidance track; selections containing different track types move horizontally only. A move that would overlap another clip on the same row or word track is blocked with a warning. Each successful group drag creates one undo step.

Delete or **Delete Selection** removes all selected cues and words. Escape restores an active drag and its undo history, or clears selection when idle. Timing fields, blend handles, right-drag blending, and **Replace** affect the active cue. Word edges resize the active word. Moving words changes guidance only; use **Regenerate from Words** to update visemes from the new word timing. The waveform and ruler retain playhead scrubbing, and yellow handles retain audio trimming.

Right-drag anywhere on a viseme or expression cue. Initial vertical movement selects strength: up increases influence, down decreases it. Initial horizontal movement selects blending: a click in the left half edits blend-in, and a click in the right half edits blend-out. Small movement is ignored while direction is detected; the chosen control stays fixed until release. Adjustments use the initial click position and cue values, even when the pointer crosses the midpoint. The horizontal yellow bar shows strength from zero at the bottom to full at the top.

**Lock blends to maximum (B)** in Selected Cue keeps each blend at half the active cue's duration, following resizing and expression holds. **B** toggles all selected viseme and expression cues; a mixed selection becomes fully locked, and pressing B again unlocks it. Locked cues show a B marker. Adjusting either blend manually unlocks that cue while preserving the other blend's current length. Strength edits keep the lock intact. Lock state is saved with cues and retained by undo/redo, cancellation, and transfer.

## Transfer

Expand **Transfer Organiser** below the timeline. It contains both cue copying and character transfer controls.

To reuse cues for another voice clip, open the destination clip and choose a source from the current character's list or load a source `LipSyncClip` in the resource picker. Select **Copy visemes**, **Copy expressions**, or both, then use **Copy into Open Clip**. Selected tracks are replaced with independent copies across all four rows, retaining strength, curves, and expression mixing. Audio, transcript, word guidance, trim, and playback mode remain with the destination. Missing destination pose mappings block copying and are listed in the status message. Copying creates one undo step; use **Save Clip** to persist the result.

**Fit timing to destination audible range** scales cue times and blend durations between the two audible ranges, retaining expression tails. Leave it unchecked to keep source timing. **Copy blending defaults and smoothing** also copies defaults for the selected cue types and overall smoothing.

For character migration, select a target profile in the organiser and use **Transfer to Character…**. The editor duplicates cue timing and transcript, assigns the target profile, and reports pose IDs absent from the target. The original clip stays with its source character. Audio can be changed in the transferred clip.

Waveform and silence analysis support WAV, Ogg Vorbis, and MP3. WAV analysis includes both stereo channels and embedded PCM samples; compressed streams are decoded without playing them aloud. **Refresh Waveform** rebuilds the display and fits the timeline without changing cue timing or audible trim. Cleared waveform data reloads automatically. The waveform row reports decoding failures or audio outside the current view instead of remaining blank. The draft generator is isolated in `editor/lip_sync_draft.gd` so a later aligner can produce the same clip cues.
